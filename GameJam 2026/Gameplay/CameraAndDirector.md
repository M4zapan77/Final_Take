# Gameplay camera and read-only movie direction

The original third-person Blueprint, animation Blueprint, skeleton and imported character remain intact. Runtime story settings cap the existing 600 cm/s walk at 500 cm/s, improve stopping/turn response, and disable character push forces on physics props. The original settings are restored when leaving story mode. The spring arm uses 400 cm distance, small shoulder/height offsets, bounded positional lag, responsive rotation and a 12 cm collision probe. Actor switching captures the actual previous camera POV before possession changes, blends for 0.35 seconds, and retains each role's viewing direction.

MovieDirectorComponent creates one transient CineCameraActor while preserving MovieManager's approved original wide camera. It selects establishing, role tracking, interaction, existing rail and final outcome shots from the chronological scene events. Significant events have priorities; shots and switches default to a 2.4-second minimum. Static visibility checks fall back to the original wide angle. No movement, scene timing, physics, objectives or scores are changed by the director.

The existing cinema screen/capture follows MovieManager.GetPremiereCamera, so directed shots work both inside the cinema and with full-screen playback. Cinematic settings remain Blueprint-editable on MovieDirectorComponent. Disabling it restores fixed-camera presentation. The visible rail and dolly use their existing shared-clock movement; no new map or camera asset was saved.

Validation: Development Editor build succeeded; 62 automated tests passed, including save/restore of the original movement and spring-arm settings. Native civilian betrayal with an actual throw and native six-failed-turn movies passed two complete film playbacks each. The driver verifies selected cinematic shots, unchanged outcome/score, exact final recorded prop poses, no movie physics simulation and clean new-movie state. Screenshots were inspected in the existing cinema. Final packaged input/performance testing remains a later milestone.
